01 / Introduction

About the company

Hyperwood is an emerging blockchain ecosystem for builders and the community that grows around them.

Product facts to confirm

The clarity of a consumer finance app. The openness of a public network.

Hyperwood is being built as an EVM-compatible network, so teams can bring the contracts, wallets, and tooling they already know and give the people who use them a clearer experience than they are used to onchain. The identity reflects that pairing: an expressive, continuous symbol inside a precise, quiet system.

In public copy, describe Hyperwood by what it does for builders and users, in plain words, and support every technical claim with a source. Where a fact below is marked to confirm, keep it out of published material until it is approved.

Builders

Developers and teams deploying applications. They want familiar tools, clear documentation, and a network that behaves predictably.

Users

People transacting through Hyperwood products. They want to understand what is about to happen before they confirm it.

Community

Contributors, ambassadors, and newcomers. They want a useful next step and a brand they can represent without guessing.

Details to confirm before publishing factual copy

  • Network architecture and the Ethereum relationship (sidechain, rollup, or other)
  • EVM compatibility statement and the launch environment (testnet, mainnet, dates)
  • Available products, developer links, and official domains
  • Token details, if any, and how they may be referenced
  • Approved company details, legal name, and public messaging

Website and transaction screens shown later in this guide are visual studies, not a deployed product.

02 / Brand foundation

Mission

Why Hyperwood exists, in one sentence everyone can repeat.

Draft for approval

Give builders a dependable network to build on, and give everyone else a clear way in.

Brand idea

Connection in motion

The Morph symbol is one continuous line that keeps moving. Hyperwood connects people, tools, and value, and it should feel alive rather than static.

Design principle

Liquid in expression. Clear in use.

Expression lives in the symbol, the graphic devices, and campaign surfaces. Everything people read, compare, or confirm stays precise and quiet.

The mission is written to be true before launch and after it. It names what Hyperwood offers without making claims about speed, cost, or scale that still need evidence.

02 / Brand foundation

Value propositions

One promise per audience. Each promise is paired with the kind of proof it needs before it appears in public copy.

Draft for approval

For builders

Familiar tools, fewer obstacles.

Bring the contracts, wallets, and workflows you already use. Documentation explains each network term the first time it appears.

Proof: EVM compatibility statement, deploy guide, tooling list

For users

Readable transactions.

Amounts, addresses, network, and status are shown in plain, legible form at every step, with a full-value copy action.

Proof: product screens, confirmation flow, error recovery

For the community

A network you can take part in.

Open documentation, visible progress, and a useful next step for newcomers: explore, read the docs, join the community.

Proof: public roadmap, release notes, community channels

How to write a proposition

  • Lead with what the person gets, then how Hyperwood delivers it.
  • Use a measurable claim only with its measurement: [value] on [network], measured [method], [date].
  • No superlatives without a source. "Fastest", "cheapest", and "most secure" are not brand language.

02 / Brand foundation

Tone of voice

The same voice everywhere, with the volume adjusted to the surface. Campaign copy can be bold. Product copy is calm.

Clear: say the thing, then the term

Avoid

Leverage our next-gen execution layer to maximize throughput for your dApp.

Prefer

Deploy your contract on Hyperwood with the tools you already use. (A contract is a program that runs on the network.)

Inviting: always offer the next step

Avoid

Documentation is available.

Prefer

Start with the docs. The first guide takes about ten minutes.

Measured: evidence instead of adjectives

Avoid

The fastest, most secure chain ever built. Don't miss out!

Prefer

Blocks confirmed in [measured time] on [network] over [period]. See the method.

By surface

CampaignShort, bold, one message. Sentence case. Ends with a period.
WebsitePlain explanations, one clear action per section.
ProductCalm and literal. Buttons say what happens next.
DocsPrecise, step-based, terms defined on first use.
SocialOne idea per post. Context sentence, then the link.

Mechanics

  • Sentence case for headlines, labels, and buttons.
  • Numbers as digits with a space before units: 2 s, 12 MB, 0.25 ETH.
  • Addresses and identifiers in mono, shortened as 0x12…89ab, with a copy action for the full value.
  • No exclamation marks in product copy. No emoji in official channels.
  • No countdowns, urgency, or price talk.

03.1 / Logo

The logo

A continuous shape with a distinctive crest and curling terminal. One connected form creates the brand signature.

Master symbol

Cue 01

The tall first crestThe highest point of the form, rising from the left pool.

Cue 02

The low central bendThe deepest valley, which gives the mark its rhythm.

Cue 03

The rounded right curlThe terminal that turns back on itself and closes the gesture.

Master artwork

Use the supplied SVG. Keep the contour, orientation, and proportions intact. The mark is a filled silhouette with no strokes, no gradients, and no effects. If a format cannot take vectors, use the 2048 px transparent PNG from the kit.

03.1 / Logo

Construction grid

The symbol sits on an eight-unit grid. Height is 8 units; width is just under 18. The three recognition cues fall on the grid, which is how a reproduction can be checked.

What the grid is for

The grid describes the master; it does not redraw it. Use it to check an embroidery digitization, a signage cut file, or a hand-painted mural against the supplied SVG. If a reproduction misses a cue, fix the reproduction, not the artwork.

Print reproduction

RGB ≠ ink

The digital neon is #CCFF00. A CMYK conversion will look duller. Ask the printer for a fluorescent spot-ink drawdown and approve it on the final material. For process-only jobs, use the printer's ICC conversion and approve a proof. Use 100K for small black text. No Pantone or universal CMYK equivalence is assigned.

:root {
  --hw-neon: #CCFF00;  --hw-ink: #0A0A0A;
  --hw-white: #FFFFFF; --hw-paper: #F4F5F0;
  --hw-slate: #62685D; --hw-line: #D9DCD2;
  --hw-success: #177A46; --hw-warning: #A86200;
  --hw-danger: #B42318;
}

Contrast, computed live (WCAG 2.2 relative luminance)

Body text needs at least 4.5:1. Large text (24 px, or 19 px bold) needs at least 3:1. Pair every state color with a label or icon.

Setting rules

  • Sentence case everywhere except mono metadata, which is uppercase.
  • Left-aligned. Centered only for a single line on a cover or tile.

03.4 / Imagery

Imagery

Two kinds of pictures: liquid material that extends the symbol's gesture, and candid photographs of people building and sharing.

Liquid material

One continuous form, never fragments. Obsidian surface with neon picked up as reflected light, not as a glow. Let darkness take most of the frame so the form reads as a gesture, like the symbol.

Human context

Candid, licensed images of people building and sharing. Real settings and natural light over staged finance imagery. No props that say "crypto"; let the caption do that.

Keep logos flat

The symbol never becomes 3D, glossy, or lit. Liquid artwork sits next to the logo, not inside it. On merch the mark is a flat screen print.

Crops and placement

Full-bleed on covers and website heroes, cropped so the brightest edge points toward the headline. In documents, use it sparingly on dividers only.

03.4 / Imagery

AI prompts

Starting prompts for generated artwork, written from the directions that produced the kit's images. Generated images show art direction; they never stand in for a real product, event, or person.

01 · Liquid material

One continuous glossy obsidian form with neon #CCFF00 reflected light, broad elastic surfaces, dark negative space, no text or logos. Studio render, soft falloff, 16:9, matte black background, subtle film grain.

Negative: glow, lens flare, particles, multiple objects, blue or purple light, text, logo, wireframe, grid.

02 · Physical applications

Tactile matte black tote bag, neon #CCFF00 notebook, business cards, and a black lanyard arranged on light concrete in a daylight studio, three-quarter overhead angle, natural shadows. The tote carries one flat neon screen-printed abstract symbol; no other text or logos.

Negative: gloss, embossing, metallic, mockup frames, hands, visible brand names, extra graphics.

03 · Human context

Candid documentary photograph of two people reviewing something on a laptop in a shared workspace, natural window light, real setting, muted colors, 35 mm lens, shallow depth of field, no visible screens with charts.

Negative: stock-photo smiles, suits, coins, rockets, holograms, neon lighting, blue tint, posed group shots.

Working with generated images

  • Composite the real SVG logo afterwards; never let a model draw the symbol.
  • Color-grade to the palette: #CCFF00 highlights, #0A0A0A shadows, no color casts.
  • Label generated images as concepts in internal decks. Use licensed photography for anything presented as real.

03.4 / Imagery

Restrictions

What Hyperwood imagery is not.

Do not use

  • Stock finance clichés: coins, candlestick charts on glass, handshakes, rockets, padlocks.
  • 3D, extruded, or chrome versions of the symbol or wordmark.
  • Neon glow, light leaks, or lens flares on type.
  • Generated people presented as team members, customers, or partners.
  • Blue, purple, or multicolor "web3" gradients and grids.

Always

  • License every photograph of a person and keep the release with the asset.
  • Keep one focal point per image and leave quiet space for the message.
  • Place type on a solid field, never directly over a busy area of the image.
  • Credit photographers where the platform allows.

03.5 / Patterns & textures

Patterns

The flow repeat: the symbol repeated at a consistent scale in a half-drop grid. The spacing establishes the rhythm; nothing else is added.

Colorway

Geometry

Tile 510 × 460 at 100%. Marks 449 px wide, horizontal pitch 510, row pitch 230, half-drop offset 310. Marks never touch.

Scale

Pick one scale per surface. 60 to 80% for banners and backgrounds; 100 to 140% for merch and large print where the shape should be recognizable.

Colorways

Single color marks on a single color field. The four pairings here are the only ones. Keep the official logo separate from the pattern.

03.5 / Patterns & textures

Textures

Texture lives in photography and physical material, not in the graphic system. Three references.

Glossy obsidian

Reserved for liquid artwork and 3D renders. Neon appears as reflected light with soft falloff, never as an emissive glow.

Matte black fabric

Totes, lanyards, apparel. The mark is a flat neon screen print; texture comes from the material, not the artwork.

Uncoated paper

Cards, letterhead, notebooks. Ink text at 100K; neon as a fluorescent spot ink approved on a drawdown.

03.5 / Patterns & textures

Usage

Where the repeat belongs, and where it does not.

Use the pattern for

  • Backgrounds behind a short headline or a single logo, with a solid field under the type.
  • Event banners, booth walls, wrapping paper, notebook end papers, apparel linings.
  • Section dividers in decks and documents, at one consistent scale.

Keep it away from

  • Body text, data tables, forms, and transaction screens.
  • Rotated, mirrored, or randomly scattered marks. The grid is the pattern.
  • Mixing colorways within one surface, or placing the official logo inside the repeat.
  • Applying textures to the logo or type. Textures stay in imagery and materials.

03.7 / Illustrations

Illustrations

Hyperwood has no separate illustration style. The master contour is scaled, cropped, and repeated to build recognition. Three graphic devices cover every need.

When to use which

Current crop for covers, posters, and social where the shape is the message. Flow repeat for backgrounds, banners, and materials. Open field for pages that carry a headline or copy.

Building a new crop

Scale the master uniformly to 2 to 6 times the frame height, move it so one of the three cues is visible, and anchor the rest off an edge. Export at the frame size from the kit SVG.

Liquid artwork

The rendered obsidian form in Imagery is the photographic counterpart of these devices. Use one or the other on a surface, not both.

03.7 / Illustrations

Restrictions

The devices stay close to the master. Anything that looks like a new drawing is out.

Also avoid

Gradients, shading, or outlines on the contour · placing the official logo inside a crop at a competing size · crops that hide all three recognition cues · textures or photographs clipped inside the shape · mirroring the contour to fit a layout.

Restrictions

Layouts fail by adding, rarely by leaving out.

Avoid

  • More than one focal point on a surface, or two contours at competing scale.
  • Centered layouts for anything longer than a single line.
  • Spacing values outside the scale, or margins tighter than the clear space rule.
  • Mixed radii on one surface, and pills on anything that is not a short action.
  • Decorative lines, boxes, and badges that do not carry information.

Check before shipping

  • Does the logo have at least x of clear space, and preferably more?
  • Is every measurement on the 8 px scale?
  • Is reading content on a neutral surface?

A circle of the same construction morphs into the silhouette in 900 ms. Once per session, on the hero, openers, splash screens, and video stings. Never looped.

02 · Rise · default

Fade and lift

Opacity 0 to 1 with a 12 px lift over 600 ms. The everyday reveal for the mark, cards, and sections. This is the kit's animated SVG.

03 · Flow · openers

A wipe that follows the gesture

The silhouette is revealed left to right, following the direction of the shape, in 600 ms. For section openers and transitions between scenes.

The curve

Easingcubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1)
Morph900 ms · path interpolation, 98 segments
Rise600 ms · opacity 0 → 1, translateY 12 px → 0

Rules

  • The mark animates as one object and always ends as the exact master.
  • Play once per view. Ambient drift (4 s, a few pixels) is allowed on hero surfaces only.
  • No bounce, spin, stretch, pixelation, particle builds, or path drawing of the outline.
  • No morphing between the mark and letters, icons, or other shapes. The drop is the only start state.
  • No decorative motion near amounts or confirmation steps.
When prefers-reduced-motion is set, show the resolved mark with no animation. Every demo on this site does.

03.9 / Motion

Ambient surfaces

Two living backgrounds for hero and opening moments: the liquid field, an animated version of the brand's imagery direction, and current lines, a quieter alternative for loading and event screens.

New in 1.1

Liquid field

Current lines

Syncing blocks…

What they are

Liquid field renders the imagery direction live: broad continuous surfaces, glossy dark material, neon as reflected light, never as a glow. It is imagery, not a color. It never fills a button, a card, or a letterform.

Current lines are thin neon lines drifting as slow waves on Ink. They carry loading states, empty states, and event screens where the liquid field would be too rich.

Both run at half resolution, cap at 30 frames per second, pause when off screen, and freeze to a single frame when reduced motion is requested.

Allowed on

  • Website hero and chapter openers, splash and loading screens, event and video backgrounds, booth screens.
  • Under a headline and one action only. Text sits on the darkest region; use the text-safe fade.
  • Neon coverage stays under about a quarter of the surface. One surface per screen.

Never on

  • Data, tables, forms, transaction flows, documentation, or anything people read for more than a moment.
  • Inside the logo, inside type, or as a fill for interface elements. The gradient rule in Color still stands for UI.
  • At full brightness, with added blur, bloom, particles, or a second color.

03.9 / Motion

Transitions

Three durations and one curve cover the whole system. Interaction is brief, reveals are gentle, ambience is slow.

Interaction

180 ms

Hover, press, focus, toggles. A brief color or opacity change, nothing moves.

Reveal

600 ms

Panels, cards, the logo, page sections. One movement, soft stop.

Ambient

4000 ms

Slow drift on hero artwork only. Alternates, never loops abruptly.

Page and view transitions

  • Content fades and rises 12 px on entry; it never slides in from the side.
  • Stagger siblings by 40 to 60 ms, up to five items, then stop staggering.
  • Exit faster than entry: 180 ms fade out. Modals scale from 98% with the same curve.
  • Scroll-linked motion is limited to parallax of the hero mark, never of text.

04 · Wait

Syncing blocks…

Waiting states breathe at the ambient tempo. Progress is a neon wipe. No spinners, no bouncing dots.

Product rules

  • Motion explains cause and effect: a row appears where it was added, a panel opens from the control that opened it.
  • Status changes use a 180 ms color change plus a label change, in that order.
  • Amounts, addresses, fees, and confirmation buttons never move, count, pulse, or shimmer.
  • No confetti, coin showers, or celebratory bursts after a transaction. A confirmed state is enough.
  • No ambient surfaces behind data. Dashboards sit on flat Ink or Paper.

Accessibility

  • Honor prefers-reduced-motion: counters show final values, charts appear drawn, lists appear in place.
  • Nothing flashes more than three times a second. Ambient tempo is the fastest loop allowed.
  • Every animated state has a text equivalent: "Syncing", "Confirmed", "Failed".
